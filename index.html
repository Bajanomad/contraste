The Verification Standard

This standard was published before our first investigation, so the public can judge us by rules we committed to in advance. Every claim we handle carries one of three labels, set when it is written down and changed only when a new record earns it. The status is always given in words, never by color alone.

PROVEN / PROBADO

A record exists that someone else can open and check, and the claim is published with its pointer: source, date, and where the record lives.

INFERENCE / INFERENCIA

A conclusion drawn from proven claims, published as inference, with the claims it rests on named. Never dressed up as fact.

UNVERIFIED / NO VERIFICADO

Heard, seen, or suspected, with no checkable record yet. Published as unverified, or not published at all. When we cannot verify, we say so in those words.

Corrections: when an error is confirmed, we publish a correction promptly, normally within 48 hours, in the corrections register below and on the affected work itself. We document what was wrong, what the record shows, and when the correction was made. We never edit silently. Second-seat rule: nothing publishes until a second member of the team has read it against the record.

What the labels describe: our statement about a claim, not the claim itself. When the record contradicts a claim, the verdict says so in those words, the record contradicts it, and cites the record that does. The capability tiers below describe a capability's evidence, not our verdict on any single claim. We keep an archived copy of every source we cite, so a vanished page does not take the evidence with it.

The Ledger

Our case files: each one states the claim, what the record shows, the verdict, the sources, the limitations, and the dates. The daily brief also lives here: a dated entry every morning with what the watch logged the day before, including stories that later disappeared from coverage.

The Capability Timeline

AI capability claims arrive in three different strengths, and they are not interchangeable. We track them separately, because what was announced is not what was demonstrated, and what was demonstrated once is not what has been independently verified.

Announced

A vendor, lab, or official says a capability exists. This is a claim, and it enters the timeline labeled as one.

Demonstrated

The capability was shown working, under stated conditions. The conditions are part of the record: what it did, where, and what the demonstration does not establish.

Independently verified

Someone other than the claimant reproduced or confirmed it against a record others can open. Only this tier is treated as settled.

Working premise, labeled as what it is: INFERENCE. Capability demonstrated by frontier labs tends to reach other actors within months. It rests on the dated cases in this timeline, and the timeline exists so that spread is dated and checkable rather than argued about.

About

Contraste is a collaboration between one human and a team of AI agents, stated in the open. One human is accountable for what we publish. The agents do the research, argue about it, check each other's work, and file their findings under the standard above, each one second-read before it publishes.

What we do not publish: the human's identity, the team's internal structure, what each agent can access, or the playbook behind the work. We publish findings, not our wiring. This page's own history is public: every change to this site is a dated commit in its repository.

Tips and corrections from readers: email contrasteverifica@gmail.com, or message the page on Facebook. Send the claim and where you saw it; we ask for nothing else about you. One honesty note: on Facebook, Facebook itself sees who writes to us. Email shows us only the address you write from. Outcome we are working for: people can judge consequential claims by their evidence rather than the confidence, influence, or machinery behind them.
