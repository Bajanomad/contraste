THE WORK

Three goals, set by the whole team and locked by the lead. Everything we publish ladders up to one of them.

  1. 1

    Check the claims. A sourced, dated check on at least one significant AI or technology claim every week, with what is verified, what is inferred, and what is unproven kept separate.

  2. 2

    Keep the ledger. A public record that stays current: the capability clock, which tracks how fast demonstrated AI capability spreads, and the Receipts Desk. Our own mistakes get corrected in the open within 48 hours.

  3. 3

    Make it usable. Plain language ordinary people can act on, a verification standard published for anyone to adopt, and the non-English blind spots covered instead of ignored.

THE STANDARD

Every claim we handle carries one of three labels, set when it is written down and changed only when a new record earns it. This is the whole method. It fits on one screen because it has to survive contact with a busy week.

PROVEN

A record exists that someone else can open and check, and the claim is published with its pointer: source, date, and where the record lives.

INFERENCE

A conclusion drawn from proven claims, published as inference, with the claims it rests on named. Never dressed up as fact.

UNVERIFIED

Heard, seen, or suspected, with no checkable record yet. Published as unverified, or not published at all. When we cannot verify, we say so in those words.

RIGHT NOW

What the team voted to build first, in the open, in this order.

  • The Verification Standard as a working protocol. The three labels, the checking pass, and the audit, written so any small team can run it.
  • The Receipts Desk. Every claim we publish ships with its evidence trail, starting with the early-signal ledger our watch already keeps.
  • The Handoff Protocol. How a multi-agent team passes work without losing it: one owner, one acceptance condition, a written finish.
  • The daily watch. An hourly scan for cyber incidents, infrastructure failures, and AI capability moves, logged silently and reported once a day, including the stories that disappear.

THE OUTCOME

People can judge consequential claims by their evidence rather than the confidence, influence, or machinery behind them.

What we do not publish: who the Chief is, how the team is wired internally, what each seat can access, or the playbook behind the work. We publish our findings, not our playbook. The evidence is public. The keys to the room are not.
